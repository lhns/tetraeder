## From ADR 0041 (addendum "2026-11-09: nightly fault testbed on CPU runners", first run, moved verbatim)

First testbed run (2026-11-09, 4-core runner, 33 runs): single top-1 12/18 (cpu 6/6, disk 6/6, mem 0/6), no-fault false positives 0/6, pair top-3 0/6. Two findings, both left open:

- `mem` is not detected although the signal is real (swap-in 190 MB/s, decode 5x slower). The detector assumes paging shows up as host time; here the stall is inside compute (0.48 s/token compute, 0.002 host), so the `host` share test fails. The Swapping rule needs a different share test (swap-in rate with the slowdown on cpu or wall time).
- A strong CPU co-tenant (2 busy loops on 4 threads, 9x slower) hides the other fault in a pair: the engine becomes CPU-bound, the disk share falls below material (0.017 s/token). The pair schedule needs a lighter co-tenant, or the pair bar needs the second fault to be material first.

## From ADR 0041 (addendum "2026-11-09: Swapping rule and pair schedule after the first testbed run", moved verbatim)

- **Diagnosis, `mem`.** The sampler already had the evidence: swap-in 183 to 206 MB/s in all six `mem` runs, 0 in every other single run. The stall lands where the swapped pages are touched: compute 0.008 to 0.50 s/token, CPU busy 0.16 to 0.40 s/token, host only 0.0009 to 0.002 (0.3% of the step), so the old `host` share test (10%) never passed.
- **Swapping rule.** Paging is swap-in at 5 MB/s or more (was 20), or major faults at that rate with a PSI memory stall of at least 1% (without it, major faults also count first reads of a mapped file). It is a fault when there is a stall: PSI memory "some" of the engine's cgroup at 10% or more, or `cpu` or `host` material and slower than calibrated (or uncalibrated). Cost: the larger of the PSI share and the excess of `cpu` and `host` over their calibrated rates (half their share when uncalibrated). The fault's resource is `ram`; it claims `cpu` and `host`, so the residual check does not report the same slowdown as a model mismatch. The rate threshold is low because the rate falls with the pace it slows and under an `io.max` that also caps swap-in; the stall requirement keeps stray pages out (sim healthy noise up to 2 MB/s, every real non-`mem` run 0).
- **Simulator.** Swapping now slows `cpu` and `host` 3x (was `host` 8x) at a swap-in of 180 to 220 MB/s, sets major faults to match, and reports a tenth of the stall as PSI (real PSI saw 4% to 5% at a 5x slowdown), on even seeds only, so runs with and without PSI both occur. Battery (3 fixtures, every fault, pair and no-fault, 10 seeds): every single and pair 10/10, no-fault 0/10 with a fault on every fixture; bars (9/10, at most 1) kept.
- **Diagnosis, pairs: a schedule problem, not ranking.** In the pairs the injected disk cap had no cost: with 2 busy loops the engine decoded at 1.34 s/token with the cap and 1.36 without, reading 64 MB/token at 48 MB/s against a 201 MB/s cap, and the measured read rate was 4 GB/s (prefetch hidden behind compute). No disk evidence exceeded the band, and the detector found at most one fault, so the top 3 had room: a ranking change cannot help. The testbed now uses 1 busy loop in a pair (half the threads alone) and scales a pair's disk cap by the partner's single-run pace over the calibration pace (cpu+disk 23 MB/s, disk+mem 44 MB/s on the first run's numbers).
- **Effect on the first run's observations** (same files, new rule): single top-1 18/18 (was 12/18), no-fault false positives 0/6, pairs 2/6 (the cpu+mem pairs; the disk pairs carry no disk evidence).
- **Second run** (nightly on `track-af-detector-fix`, run 42656840809, a slower 4-core runner: 0.25 s/token without fault; observations re-derived with the fixed sampler in `bench/results/20261109-runner/detector-testbed-2/`, CI score in `score-ci.json`). The new schedule works: cpu+disk now 2/2 (disk cap 19 MB/s, both found). The first version of the rule, with PSI deciding when present, scored single 12/18, pairs 2/6: memory PSI was only 4% to 5% at a 5x slowdown (swap-in 111 to 120 MB/s), and swap-in fell to 17 MB/s under the disk+mem cap. Hence PSI as a floor rather than a gate and the 5 MB/s rate above. Final rule on this run's observations, rescored offline: single top-1 18/18, no-fault false positives 0/6, pairs 6/6. On the testbed the calibration has a rate only for the disk, so `cpu` is always uncalibrated and material (its threads spin): the Swapping stall check always passes there and the swap-in rate alone separates `mem` (17 to 120 MB/s) from everything else (0). CpuCotenant has the same property. A CPU work-unit counter would let the calibration carry a CPU rate.
